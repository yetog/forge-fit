# Sunday Transfer Session

Today, I'd avoid turning this into a mechanics marathon. You already said you want to play some holes and enjoy Sunday. So make today a transfer day: a little deliberate practice, then golf.

## Today's session: ~20 minutes practice → play

### 1. 5 minutes — slow-motion rehearsals, no ball

Do your five-step sequence:

SPACE → CHARGE → DROP → TURN → BALANCE

Go ridiculously slow. Maybe 10–15 reps.

The main thing I want you feeling is the transition:

Charge → tiny transition/drop → turn through.

Don't practice consciously snapping the wrists or forcing a release today. Let the club respond to the sequence.

### 2. 10 minutes — build from short to long

Hit roughly 3–5 balls at each stage:

Wedge → 7i → 4i → Driver

Start around 50–60% effort and gradually work toward your normal swing.

For every shot, only grade three things:

1. Did I create enough space at address?
2. Did I drop before aggressively turning?
3. Could I hold my finish for 3 seconds?

Don't make ball flight the grade yet. A mediocre shot with the movement you're training can be more useful than a beautiful shot produced by your old compensation.

### 3. 5 minutes — driver experiment

This is where you can use the launch monitor.

Don't chase speed. You've already demonstrated 106 mph, so we know speed exists.

Today, chase repeatability of path.

Your old default was around -12°, and you've already produced approximately -2°. So I'd consider:

| Path | Evaluation |
|------|------------|
| -12° | old pattern |
| -8° | improvement |
| -5° | good progress |
| -3° to +2° | excellent practice window |

Hit a ball, look at the path, then ask yourself what that swing felt like. If you get -2°, don't immediately try to improve it. Reproduce it. That's the experiment.

---

## Then go play

Once you start playing holes, turn the laboratory off.

No:

"Okay, trail elbow down, wrist angle, hip snap, torso rotation, don't rise, swing plane…"

That's going to fry you.

Before the shot, do one rehearsal of your sequence. Then over the actual ball I'd give yourself one cue only:

"Charge. Drop. Turn."

Then hit it.

And today I'd actually keep score in two different ways. Keep your normal golf score, but mentally give yourself a ✔️ whenever you commit to your routine and finish balanced — regardless of where the ball ends up.

That's directly connected to what you were saying earlier about discipline. Today isn't about proving you've fixed your swing. It's about beginning to turn the swing you've discovered into a routine you can reproduce without consciously controlling every body part.

And if the driver suddenly reads -10/-12 again, don't spend 30 balls fighting it. Go back to a couple slow rehearsals/half swings, find the movement again, and continue. Your upcoming lesson is where I'd do the deeper mechanical diagnosis.
