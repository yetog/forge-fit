# 5-Day Golf Practice System

Yeah. Given where your swing is right now, I'd make the five days serve different purposes so you're not standing there five days a week endlessly tweaking mechanics.

I'd use Monday / Wednesday / Friday as the main 60-minute sessions, with Tuesday / Thursday as lighter 30-minute sessions. That gives you 4 hours/week of deliberate practice.

The whole program is built around your current priorities: make the newer neutral-ish path repeatable, improve long-club face/contact, preserve your already-good short-club motion, and eventually stop thinking mechanically while playing.

## Your 5-day golf practice system

| Day | Time | Purpose |
|-----|------|---------|
| Monday | 60 min | Mechanics + swing-plane development |
| Tuesday | 30 min | Short game + scoring |
| Wednesday | 60 min | Contact + club progression |
| Thursday | 30 min | Calibration + consistency |
| Friday | 60 min | Performance / simulated golf |

---

## Monday — Swing Lab

60 minutes — technical day

This is your scientist day. It's the one day where I want you looking at club path and experimenting.

### 0–10 min — Movement

No concern about ball flight yet.

Do slow rehearsals of:

SPACE → CHARGE → DROP → TURN → BALANCE

Then wedge → 7i at 50–70%.

Especially rehearse Charge → Drop → Turn.

### 10–25 min — Path calibration

Use 7i/4i.

Start with half swings and work toward full.

You're looking for the movement that moves you away from that old ~-12° path toward the neutral neighborhood you've already demonstrated.

Don't obsess over exactly -2.0°. Something like -4°, -3°, -1°, +1° can all produce golf shots depending on face.

The experiment is:

What movement reliably produces my desired path?

Record the number and the feel.

### 25–40 min — Driver laboratory

Now driver.

Start ~70% speed. Forget the 106-mph number temporarily.

Monitor:

Club path → attack angle → ball speed → ball flight

If path goes crazy again, reduce speed rather than inventing a new swing.

Once you're producing the motion, gradually bring speed back.

### 40–50 min — Face/contact

Now stop chasing path.

Use impact spray/foot powder if available.

Where are you striking the driver face?

You want to understand whether your right miss is coming from face orientation, strike location, or both.

### 50–60 min — Transfer

Turn the monitor around or stop looking at numbers.

Pick targets.

One rehearsal.

SPACE → CHARGE → DROP → TURN

Hit.

Hold the finish.

You're now a golfer, not an engineer.

---

## Tuesday — Scoring Lab

30 minutes — completely different

Very little swing mechanics.

You're protecting yourself from becoming the guy with a beautiful TrackMan swing who can't score. 😂

- 5 min: putting speed control
- 10 min: chips/pitches from different distances
- 10 min: wedge targets — 30/50/70/90-ish yards
- 5 min: random challenge

For the final challenge, never hit the same shot twice.

Different target. Different distance. Different club.

Your metric today isn't club path.

It's:

How close did the ball finish to what I intended?

---

## Wednesday — Build the Bag

60 minutes — consistency across club length

This directly attacks the pattern you've noticed:

- wedges/short irons = straight
- long irons/driver = right miss

We're going to find where your swing changes.

Start with 10 minutes of warm-up, then spend about 35 minutes climbing the bag:

Wedge → 9i → 7i → 5i → 4i/hybrid → wood → driver

Only hit about 3–5 balls per club before moving.

You're studying:

At what club length does my pattern deteriorate?

Don't immediately fix it.

Observe it.

If wedge through 7i are beautiful and 5i suddenly starts leaking right, that's useful evidence.

Then spend 10 minutes going backward:

Driver → 4i → 7i → wedge.

That's a surprisingly useful test because now you're seeing whether your good motion survives after hitting long clubs.

Finish with 5 minutes of random clubs.

Driver.
8i.
Wedge.
4i.
7i.

No rhythm from hitting the same club 15 times.

That's much closer to golf.

---

## Thursday — Calibration Day

30 minutes — low intensity

This is your precision day, not a workout.

Spend the first 10 minutes hitting mostly half and three-quarter swings.

Your objective:

Center contact.

Then spend 10 minutes doing your five-stage motion extremely deliberately:

SPACE
↓
CHARGE
↓
DROP
↓
TURN
↓
BALANCE

You can even pause between stages during rehearsals.

Last 10 minutes:

Pick one target.

Hit 10 balls.

Give yourself:

- 2 points — target window
- 1 point — playable miss
- 0 points — big miss

Maximum = 20 points.

Write down the score.

That's important because now Thursday gives us a weekly standardized measurement.

---

## Friday — Play Golf

60 minutes — NO SWING LAB

Friday is my favorite.

You're playing imaginary holes.

Take a scorecard or visualize a course.

### Hole 1 — Par 4

Driver.

Where did it finish?

Now imagine the remaining distance.

Grab the appropriate iron.

Then wedge/chip if necessary.

Repeat.

Play 6–9 simulated holes depending on pace.

You only get one ball per shot.

That's the rule.

No:

"That sucked, let me try again."

Because golf doesn't give you Ctrl+Z.

And importantly, don't look at club-path numbers after every shot.

Your pre-shot process becomes:

Target → SPACE → one rehearsal → CHARGE/DROP/TURN → GO.

Then accept the result.

Friday tests whether Monday–Thursday actually transferred.

## The two versions

If life gets busy, don't skip practice just because you can't do the full hour.

### Your 30-minute template becomes:

- 5 min — movement/warm-up
- 10 min — day's primary skill
- 10 min — randomized/target practice
- 5 min — performance test

### Your 60-minute template becomes:

- 10 min — movement/warm-up
- 20 min — deliberate skill development
- 15 min — variable/random practice
- 10 min — performance challenge
- 5 min — record observations

So the purpose stays the same, but the volume changes.

## Golf Journal

And I want you keeping a tiny golf journal

Not paragraphs.

After every session, record:

```
DATE:
SESSION: Monday / Tuesday / etc.
BEST FEEL:
COMMON MISS:
ONE NUMBER:
WHAT WORKED:
NEXT SESSION:
```

For example:

```
Monday
Best feel: arms dropping before rotation
Common miss: high right
Club path: -3.1° average
Worked: slower transition
Next: reproduce without exaggerating drop
```

That becomes incredibly valuable after 4–6 weeks because we're no longer reasoning from memory.

We'll have an actual dataset of your golf swing.

## The Philosophy

And there's a bigger philosophy behind the schedule:

| Day | Purpose |
|-----|---------|
| Monday | Understand |
| Tuesday | Score |
| Wednesday | Generalize |
| Thursday | Calibrate |
| Friday | Perform |

That's exactly the learning system we've been talking about: analyze deeply, isolate variables, build the model, test generalization, then shut your analytical brain down and execute.
