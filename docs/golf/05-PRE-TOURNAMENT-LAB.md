# Pre-Tournament Lab

Yeah — and I'd actually change your plan slightly because tomorrow is competition mode, not skill-development mode.

Tonight you don't need to solve your entire golf swing. You need to answer a few very specific questions so that tomorrow you know what your bread-and-butter options are.

## Tonight's 30-minute "pre-tournament lab"

### 1. Find your tee-shot main and backup — ~10 minutes

Hit maybe 3–5 balls each with driver, wood, and 7/8 iron. Don't judge them by the best shot. Judge them by the dispersion of all the shots. That 260-yard wood was awesome, but because you couldn't reproduce it on the next two attempts, it isn't your tournament bread-and-butter yet. If your 8-iron gives you five playable balls and your wood gives you one bomb plus four disasters, the 8-iron wins tomorrow.

And one correction: I would not plan around driving a 300–320-yard par 4. Even if you can occasionally produce a huge shot, your strategic question tomorrow is "What leaves me a comfortable second shot without bringing trouble into play?" A 150-yard iron + 150-yard iron is 300 yards just as surely as one 300-yard drive.

### 2. Lab your approach swing — ~10 minutes

Pick the club you're most comfortable approaching with—probably PW, 9, or your approach wedge—and test one repeatable stock motion. Don't spend tonight trying to perfect full, ¾, ½ and ¼ swings simultaneously. Your biggest existing issue has been that your "half swing" tends to become too large. Find one shortened swing you can reproduce and note roughly what distance it produces.

### 3. Build one greenside shot — ~5 minutes

Your instinct to choke down for control is reasonable. Test it. But don't try to invent several kinds of chips tonight. Pick PW or your higher-lofted wedge, choke down somewhat, make a small controlled motion, and see which gives you the most predictable result. Tomorrow, use the boring shot you trust.

### 4. Finish with simulated holes — ~5 minutes

This is the important part. Stop "practicing" and play imaginary holes: 320-yard par 4 → tee club → approach club. Then another. No mulligans. One ball per shot. That's your transition from Training Mode to Ranked.

## Your swing thought tomorrow should be much simpler than your notes

You currently have way too many potential inputs to consciously execute during a shot: knee, head, vertical level, hip rotation, whip, contact, grip, follow-through, stillness, etc.

Don't take all of those onto the course.

Your pre-shot sequence can be:

**Target → practice swing → address → smooth/whip → hold finish.**

The practice swing should be your rehearsal. Step in relatively promptly while that sensation is still available and reproduce it rather than beginning another mechanical analysis.

And I'd leave the lead-knee experiment out of tomorrow's round. Your observation is useful, but a lead knee gaining some flex in the backswing and then extending as you rotate through isn't inherently a fault. What you're actually trying to diagnose is whether your knee movement changes your height, low point, or balance. That's a range/video experiment for another session. Tomorrow, your existing "hold the finish" test gives you a much simpler proxy: if you can finish balanced and hold it, don't start rebuilding your knee mechanics mid-round.

## Your decision rule tomorrow

This is where your fighting-game analogy becomes extremely useful:

**Don't choose the move with the highest theoretical damage. Choose the move with the highest expected damage that you can confirm consistently.**

| Club | Analogy |
|------|---------|
| Driver | might be your 40% combo |
| Wood | might eventually become your 35% BnB |
| 8-iron | might currently be your boring 20% punish that almost never drops |

On a short par 4 with trouble, boring can absolutely win.

And tomorrow gives you fantastic data for ForgeFit. Don't record every technical thought. Afterward, record only: which tee club produced playable shots, which approach felt most repeatable, your dominant miss, and the one swing cue that survived actual course pressure.

That's your first real practice → competition → review loop.
