# Diagnosing the Left Miss

Yeah. There are two different things in this memo that I'd separate:

1. Your pre-shot routine is becoming much clearer.
2. Your practice data is revealing a specific problem: you can produce excellent contact, but direction/control isn't repeatable yet—especially the left miss.

That second one should probably become your next ForgeFit experiment.

One technical correction first, though: I wouldn't deliberately manipulate the clubface at address to create topspin/backspin. With normal iron/wedge shots, you're generally trying to start with a square face and make clean contact; the club's loft, impact conditions, and friction produce the shot's launch/spin. For now, square + predictable beats manipulating the face.

---

## Your pre-shot routine is becoming your BnB combo

I'd condense everything you described into five checkpoints, because you don't want twelve conscious thoughts standing over the ball.

### 1. TARGET — What shot am I making?

Pick the target, distance, club, and intended trajectory. Don't just stare at the ball.

### 2. BUILD — Put your body and club in position.

Ball position → distance from ball → posture → grip → square clubface. Your posture should feel athletic and repeatable rather than trying to consciously achieve an arbitrary torso height.

### 3. REHEARSE — Practice the exact shot.

This is an important insight from your memo. Your practice swing isn't ceremonial. If you're about to hit a controlled half PW, rehearse that. If you're hitting an iron from a tee, rehearse that. Feel tempo, low point and finish.

### 4. EXECUTE — Ball becomes the only job.

Once you step in, stop rebuilding the swing. Maintain your posture, see the ball through impact, and let the club move through it.

### 5. FINISH — Stick the landing.

Rotate through, complete the follow-through, and hold the finish. Then watch/evaluate the shot.

---

## Quick Mnemonic

So your quick mnemonic could simply be:

**Target → Build → Rehearse → See → Finish.**

That's short enough to survive an actual round.

## Your next experiment: the left miss

This is where I would not immediately conclude that your hips or follow-through are causing it.

For a right-handed golfer, a ball curving left tells us something about the clubface relative to the swing path. But we need another piece of information before diagnosing it.

### Pay attention to where the ball STARTS.

| Start | Curve | What It Is |
|-------|-------|------------|
| Starts straight | curves left | That's a **hook**. Your face is closed relative to your path. |
| Starts right | curves left | That's a **push-draw / overdraw** depending on how far it moves. The face started right of the target but was closed relative to the path. |
| Starts left | stays left | That's primarily a **pull**. Different problem. |
| Starts left | curves even farther left | That's a **pull-hook**. Now you've got both starting direction and curvature working left. |

That distinction is exactly your fighting-game frame-data idea. Saying "it went left" isn't enough information to debug the input.

## Your next 30-minute ForgeFit session

Don't try to fix the knee, hips, face, grip, follow-through and direction simultaneously.

Call the session:

**Experiment: Diagnose My Left Miss**

Use your 7 or 8 iron, since you're already reasonably comfortable with them.

Hit ~20–30 controlled balls at one target.

For every shot, record only three things:

| Data Point | Options |
|------------|---------|
| Contact | clean / thin / fat |
| Start | left / center / right |
| Curve | left / straight / right |

That's it.

You might discover something like:

- 16/25 clean contact
- 12/16 clean shots started center
- 8/12 curved left

Now we have actual evidence that your contact is improving while face-to-path control is the next bottleneck.

That's much more useful than, "Maybe my hip did something."

And importantly, don't make five corrections during this diagnostic session. First establish the pattern.

## Your knee becomes a separate experiment

You've repeatedly noticed that lead-knee dip. That's worth investigating, but don't combine it with the left-miss experiment.

Later, run:

**Hypothesis: Excessive lead-knee movement changes my vertical level and low point.**

Film 10 swings from face-on if possible.

Compare:

good strike vs bad strike → knee movement → head/torso height → balance at finish.

You may discover the knee isn't the problem at all. It could simply be a visible part of a perfectly functional pressure/rotation pattern. That's why I don't want you artificially freezing your left knee just because you noticed movement.

## One particularly good thing buried in your memo

Your "half swing" appears to be becoming your stock swing.

That's okay.

Rather than forcing golf terminology onto what your body feels, start calibrating it:

| Your Feel | Actual Carry |
|-----------|--------------|
| Your ½ feel | actual carry X |
| Your ¾ feel | actual carry Y |
| Your full feel | actual carry Z |

If your subjective "half" produces 80% of your maximum distance while giving you dramatically better contact and dispersion, that's valuable information. Eventually you can rename it your stock swing instead of worrying about whether it objectively looks like a half swing.
