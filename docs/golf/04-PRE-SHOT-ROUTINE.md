# Pre-Shot Routine

Yep — you had a full sequence, and I agree it makes sense to preserve it rather than compress it. Here's your pre-shot routine laid out in the same order and with the same logic you described.

---

## Your Full Pre-Shot Routine

### 1. See the ball and picture the shot

Start by looking at the ball and deciding what shot you are actually trying to hit.

Think about:

- where you want the ball to go
- how far you need it to go
- what kind of trajectory you want
- whether this is a tee shot, iron shot, wedge shot, chip, etc.

The point is to have the shot in mind before you start worrying about mechanics.

### 2. Set your body distance from the ball

Move into position and make sure your spacing feels correct.

Pay attention to:

- how far your body is from the ball
- how much you are bending
- whether your torso feels aligned comfortably over the shot
- whether you feel balanced instead of reaching or crowding the ball

You want to find the position where the club can naturally meet the ball without you having to compensate.

### 3. Set your posture and vertical level

Once your distance is right, settle into the height/posture you want.

You've been paying attention to:

- how low you are bending
- keeping your torso stable
- not changing your vertical level excessively during the swing
- avoiding unnecessary dipping that changes where the club bottoms out

This is where your knee observation fits in too: the knee can move naturally, but you don't want that movement changing your entire body level.

### 4. Check the clubhead and face

Now look at the club itself.

Make sure:

- the clubface is lined up the way you intend
- the face is not accidentally too open or too closed
- the club looks positioned to make a clean, flush strike
- the loft of the club is being allowed to do its job

For now, the main goal is clean, square contact, not trying to manufacture spin.

### 5. Check your grip

Once the face is where you want it, make sure your grip feels right.

Your grip should:

- feel secure
- give you control of the clubface
- not be so tight that you kill the flow of the swing
- help you return the face consistently through impact

This is one of the places you've noticed that good setup can make the eventual contact feel almost automatic.

### 6. Take the first practice swing

Now you rehearse.

This first practice swing is about:

- feeling the ground
- brushing the grass lightly
- making sure your low point feels right
- hearing/feeling that "aggressive graze" rather than chopping into the turf

You're checking whether the swing is reaching the bottom where you expect it to.

### 7. Take the second practice swing and rehearse the actual shot

This one is more specific.

Think about:

- how long the swing should be
- half swing vs three-quarter swing
- how much distance you are trying to produce
- your tempo
- your follow-through
- where your arms are going after impact

For your irons and wedges, you've been finding that what feels like a "half swing" often gives you a lot of the distance you need anyway.

If the ball is on a tee, you also adjust the rehearsal to account for the fact that the ball is elevated.

### 8. Step into the real shot and recreate the practice swing

This is the part you've been trying to make more consistent.

Once the practice swing feels right:

- step in
- recreate the same setup
- recreate the same tempo
- recreate the same swing length
- don't invent a completely different swing just because a ball is now there

The goal is to transfer the rehearsal into the actual shot.

### 9. Lock your eyes on the ball through contact

Once you start the real swing, your concentration narrows.

Your job is:

- keep your eyes on the ball
- stay with it through impact
- don't look up early
- actually see/feel the strike happen

This has been one of your biggest contact cues, especially with the longer irons.

### 10. Let the downswing whip instead of forcing it

You've also noticed this is important.

You want:

- controlled acceleration
- momentum from the club
- torso and hips helping the motion
- less violent arm effort

The sensation is more like whipping the club through than trying to smash the ball with brute force.

### 11. Follow through completely

After impact:

- let the arms continue
- rotate through
- don't abruptly stop the swing
- finish the motion you rehearsed

You've compared this to holding a basketball free-throw follow-through, which is a good analogy for you.

### 12. Hold the finish and watch the ball

Once you've made contact:

- hold your finish
- stay balanced
- then track the ball flight

That gives you feedback on:

- direction
- contact
- balance
- whether the real swing matched the practice swing

## Summary

That is basically your full routine as you described it:

**visualize → position → posture → clubface → grip → ground rehearsal → shot rehearsal → recreate → see contact → whip through → follow through → hold finish.**

I'd preserve this as your actual ForgeFit "Pre-Shot Routine" because the detail is useful for learning; later, once it becomes automatic, you can naturally shorten it without losing the underlying sequence.
